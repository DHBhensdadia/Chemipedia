Element group colours

Each fill is shown with the foreground the site would choose for it, and the contrast ratio between them. Every pairing must reach WCAG AA for text; a ratio below 4.5 is a failure and the number turns to tell you so.

The living paper

The field under every page: four fixed sheets drawn in CSS and bounded by tokens §22 — the wash, the page family's bloom, the lattice and the grain. This guide carries the shipped field, so the section demonstrates itself: the blooms in these corners are the blooms in this page's corners, and the lattice under these words is the periodic table's own pitch. Bottom to top, the sheets are html::before (the coral and the amber), body::before (the family bloom and the pale blue), body::after (the lattice, faded toward the frame) and html::after (the grain). Every swatch below is the composite the tokens actually draw: the hue mixed into the paper at that bloom's own alpha, nothing remembered.

A page family names one of the pairs below — the bloom in the top-left corner — and the pages that carry no family wear the default. The drift is the field's only motion: two transforms on two long timings, compositor-only, and the reader's preference decides — In print the sheets are not drawn at all.

    Periodic table

    The engine, rendered from the real data layer in every colour mode it has. Point at or tab to a legend chip to isolate its group; tab to a tile and use the arrow keys to move through the grid. One tile is in the tab order at a time.

    The atom viewer

    The one part of the site that draws in three dimensions, proved here before any page depends on it. The real modules on a canvas of real pixels, over the real records: pick any of the 118 elements, or type the three counts, and the atom is built from that record's own shells — one instanced draw call for every particle, one small mesh per orbit, and a camera that eases toward whatever you ask for. Drag to swing it, wheel to come closer.

    The readout under the stage is measured on this machine rather than remembered: the draw calls are counted by wrapping the context before the layer acquires it, and the frame time is the scene's own work — the camera, the electrons, the uploads and the draw — averaged over a second of frames. Nothing here is asserted from another machine's numbers.
